New Concept #1

Speaker: Emmanuel Zepeda

Wage Standard

The Wage Standard is the idea that society has an accepted range of fair pay for most jobs.

  • Workers should share economic growth.
  • Fair pay standards once existed more strongly.
  • These standards weakened in the 1980s and 1990s.
  • Lower wage standards contribute to inequality.

New Concept #2

Speaker: Roberto Garcia

Monopsony Power

Monopsony occurs when employers have significant power over workers because employees have limited job alternatives.

  • Workers cannot easily leave.
  • Employers gain wage-setting power.
  • Pay remains lower than expected.
  • Low-income workers are affected most.

New Concept #3

Speaker: Roberto Garcia

The Great Divergence

The Great Divergence describes the growing separation between worker pay and economic productivity after 1980.

  • Before 1980, wages and productivity grew together.
  • After 1980, productivity kept rising.
  • Worker wages lagged behind.
  • Inequality increased dramatically.

Applying to Poverty

Wages are the main source of income for most households. When wages fail to increase with productivity, families can struggle with housing, food, healthcare, and transportation.


A growing economy does not automatically eliminate poverty. The benefits of growth must be distributed fairly.

Class Discussion

Hopeful Solutions

Higher Minimum Wages

Raises income for low-wage workers.

Stronger Worker Voice

Unions can improve bargaining power.

Fair Wage Standards

Creates shared prosperity and stability.

Tight Labor Markets

Employers compete for workers and raise wages.
